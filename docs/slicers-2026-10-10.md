# Slicers: finding automated execution programs (2026-10-10)

On 2026-10-10 from 17:08 UTC the public site's Binance ETH spot CVD row ran in a straight line: a program bought ETHUSDT with taker
orders of $1-6K about once a second, about $0.2M a minute, for hours (≈ $46M by 21:18 UTC, price +0.4 %). This note is the evidence
behind `src/shared/slicers.ts`, the detector for such programs.

## The rule

After Argus_Bot's execution-program detector (`Argus_Bot/src/microstructure/execution_program.py`, live on Binance spot since 2026-07;
its own 92-hour replay found 4 episodes, at most 2 in 24 h). Every market order from $1K (times the coin's size scale) goes into a
logarithmic size bucket; a bucket with its two free neighbours is a size family, so the clip size is found rather than set. Each minute,
over the ten minutes to its end, a family is a candidate when it jointly has enough clips and dollars a minute, at least 90 % of its clips
on one side, one most common gap between the seconds holding its clips taking at least half of the gaps, and a recent last clip. Two kinds:

| Kind | Buckets | Size test | Clips a minute | Most common gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| exact (Argus's) | 0.2 % | within 0.2 % of the median | ≥ 5 | ≤ 30 s |
| wide | 50 % (a family spans ≈ 3.4×) | none | ≥ 20 | ≤ 5 s |

Fifteen candidate minutes make an episode (onset = the first, alert = the fifteenth; up to two missed minutes in a row on the way do not
start it over); five without one end it; the same side and clip again within six hours is kept as a repeat and not announced. Inputs are the market orders the recorders rebuild from fills, so a clip
that took two price levels is one order (Binance's aggregate trades split it).

## Wavelets (Karl's question: would wavelets or the scattering transform find these better?)

`Research_Files/slicers_wst/wst_study.py` (private, ML env, kymatio 0.3.0). 1,225 ten-minute windows of the cases above with at least
5 orders a minute in the side's busiest wide family, 523 of them inside a program. ROC AUC (1 = every window right):

| Feature of the family's per-second order counts | AUC |
|---|---|
| The detector's cadence test (share of the most common gap, ≤ 5 s) | 0.974 |
| Rate (orders a minute) | 0.949 |
| Direction (share on its side) | 0.912 |
| Fano factor (low = regular) | 0.851 |
| Allan factor (Haar wavelet variance) at 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 s | 0.832, 0.798, 0.870, 0.859, 0.855, 0.841 |
| WST order 1 (6 coefficients), logistic regression leaving each case out | 0.979 |
| WST orders 1+2 (18), the same | 0.970 |
| The cadence test and direction together, the same | 0.972 |
| WST orders 1+2 on every order of the side from $1K (no size families) | 0.920 |
| ... with the side's share | 0.957 |

The scattering transform separates as well as the cadence test and no better; without the size families it is worse. The families carry
the separation, and the one-line cadence test reads the time structure as well as the wavelets do, so the detector keeps it (a TS port of
the transform would add filters and FFTs for nothing measured). A program that also randomises its timing would defeat the cadence test;
none of this week's did.

Every announced episode with the defaults was read: 26 of the 29 are fixed clips (sizes within 0.2 %) and the other three are the
randomised programs above; none is ordinary flow. Among them: a program near 07:33 UTC most days (4-second gaps for 15-30 minutes: ETH
$13.27K buy Oct 5, $10.80K sell Oct 6, $4.38K buy Oct 8; BTC $27.44K buy Oct 6, $13.24K Oct 7, $3.99K Oct 8, $9.53K Oct 9), an ETH seller
of $5.00K every 3 seconds that came back over two days (Oct 7 12:44 to Oct 9 00:11, with gaps), an ETH seller of exactly 6 ETH a clip
(about $15.45K) every second or so (Oct 7 15:42-16:16, $22.3M), a BTC $60.00K seller every 2 seconds ($29M in 20 minutes, Oct 5 09:00), a
BTC $10,003 buyer (Oct 5 10:00-10:50, $4.6M), the BTC $4.14K spot seller and perpetual buyer of Oct 10 10:08 (one program, a hedge), and a
BTC perpetual buyer of $49.95K every 5 seconds (Oct 10 17:41, $6.3M). Today's ETH buyer: onset 17:12, alert 17:26, still running at 22:00
with $52.5M on its side.

The two missed minutes allowed while an episode builds (`pendingSlack`) are what found the 6 ETH seller: it was a candidate for 14 minutes,
paused two, and was a candidate for 13 more, so fifteen in a row never came. They add 1 episode on ETH spot and 2 on BTC spot (a $10,003
and a $5,000 buyer), all fixed clips.

Of the 29 announced, 16 averaged $100K a minute or more over their candidate minutes (total / minutes from onset to last), 17 reached
$5M, and 11 did both: a size floor for sounds (a page setting, scaled with the coin) would leave about one a day per busy market. The
episode's `usdPerMin` is its last window's, not this average.

Known tradeoff: the Oct 7 overnight ETH seller now ends at 02:02 and comes back at 03:31 as a repeat, though it was selling throughout
(~$430K a minute at 03:00-03:30): its family's side fell under 75 % for a while, and coming back needs fifteen minutes at 90 % again.

What the replay cannot say: it is Binance only. Live, the detector reads every venue of the coin, including those that report each fill
(Bybit, Coinbase, Hyperliquid), where the orders are rebuilt; arbitrage takers and inventory bots on thinner venues may make one-sided,
regular flow Binance did not show here. The replay also clocks each minute on the orders' own times; live, a feed more than `GRACE_MS`
late leaves its last orders to the following windows. Direction counts clips, not dollars, so unrelated orders of a program's size count
against it (the BTC perpetual buyer read 88 %).

The wide kind's settings: 25 % buckets break today's buyer into pieces (above); on BTC spot they find only what the exact kind finds, and
their one extra on ETH spot is the 6 ETH seller, which the exact kind now finds. 60 % loses the BTC perp program. A rate of 10 or a
direction of 0.85 adds, on ETH spot, quieter stretches of the $5.00K seller (the exact kind reports it) and the 6 ETH seller. 50 %, 20 a
minute and 0.9 is the default.

Fixes the replay forced (all in the module and its tests): a running episode keeps its side only while at least 75 % of its family's
clips are on it (Argus kept it on any direction, so a busy wide family held one open after its program stopped); a repeat is the same side
and the same clip (it was any side with overlapping sizes, which made the $108M ETH seller of Oct 6 a "repeat" of a $5K buyer); a
fixed-clip episode's numbers come from its exact family only; a fixed clip goes on as the same program only within 0.5 % (a clip of
fixed dollars does not move, one of fixed coins moves with the price), and exact families are paired with episodes for the most pairs and
then the closest, one a minute (with Argus's 2.5 %, two fixed programs a few percent apart were one episode; on Oct 7 a short burst of
$5.13K clips took over the $5.00K seller's episode). The Codex reviews' constructed cases for these (programs 2 % apart starting,
stopping, moving together) are tests; the replay's episodes did not change with the last three of these fixes.
