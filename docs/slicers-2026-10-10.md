# Slicers: finding automated execution programs (2026-10-10)

On 2026-10-10 from 17:08 UTC the public site's Binance ETH spot CVD row ran in a straight line: a program bought ETHUSDT with taker
orders of $1-6K about once a second, about $0.2M a minute, for hours (≈ $46M by 21:18 UTC, price +0.4 %). This note is the evidence
behind `src/shared/slicers.ts`, the detector for such programs.

## The rule

After Argus_Bot's execution-program detector (`Argus_Bot/src/microstructure/execution_program.py`, live on Binance spot since 2026-07;
its own 92-hour replay found 4 episodes, at most 2 in 24 h). Every market order from $1K (times the coin's size scale) goes into a
logarithmic size bucket; a bucket with its two free neighbours is a size family, so the clip size is found rather than set. Each minute,
over the ten minutes to its end, a family is a candidate when it jointly has enough clips and dollars a minute, at least 90 % of its clips
on one side, one most common gap between the seconds holding its clips taking at least half of the gaps, and a recent last clip. Two kinds:

| Kind | Buckets | Size test | Clips a minute | Most common gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| exact (Argus's) | 0.2 % | within 0.2 % of the median | ≥ 5 | ≤ 30 s |
| wide | 50 % (a family spans ≈ 3.4×) | none | ≥ 20 | ≤ 5 s |

Fifteen candidate minutes in a row make an episode (onset = the first, alert = the fifteenth); five without one end it; the same sizes
again within six hours are kept as a repeat and not announced. Inputs are the market orders the recorders rebuild from fills, so a clip
that took two price levels is one order (Binance's aggregate trades split it).

## Wavelets (Karl's question: would wavelets or the scattering transform find these better?)

`Research_Files/slicers_wst/wst_study.py` (private, ML env, kymatio 0.3.0). 1,225 ten-minute windows of the cases above with at least
5 orders a minute in the side's busiest wide family, 523 of them inside a program. ROC AUC (1 = every window right):

| Feature of the family's per-second order counts | AUC |
|---|---|
| The detector's cadence test (share of the most common gap, ≤ 5 s) | 0.974 |
| Rate (orders a minute) | 0.949 |
| Direction (share on its side) | 0.912 |
| Fano factor (low = regular) | 0.851 |
| Allan factor (Haar wavelet variance) at 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 s | 0.832, 0.798, 0.870, 0.859, 0.855, 0.841 |
| WST order 1 (6 coefficients), logistic regression leaving each case out | 0.979 |
| WST orders 1+2 (18), the same | 0.970 |
| The cadence test and direction together, the same | 0.972 |
| WST orders 1+2 on every order of the side from $1K (no size families) | 0.920 |
| ... with the side's share | 0.957 |

The scattering transform separates as well as the cadence test and no better; without the size families it is worse. The families carry
the separation, and the one-line cadence test reads the time structure as well as the wavelets do, so the detector keeps it (a TS port of
the transform would add filters and FFTs for nothing measured). A program that also randomises its timing would defeat the cadence test;
none of this week's did.

## Replay

`scripts/replay-slicers.mts` drives the module over Binance's aggregate trades (cached in `data/runtime/aggtrades`, outside git) and counts
what it would announce. Spot 2026-10-03 21:00 to 10-10 22:00 UTC (ETH 1.62M taker orders, BTC 4.00M); perpetuals 10-09 01:00 to 10-10
22:00 (0.59M and 0.60M; Binance answers no further back). Announced episodes (repeats not counted), a day, and the most in any 24 hours:

| Setting | ETH spot | BTC spot | ETH perp | BTC perp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| both kinds (the defaults) | 13 (1.85/day, max 6) | 11 (1.56/day, max 4) | 0 | 2 (max 2) |
| exact only (Argus) | 10 (1.42/day, max 6) | 10 (1.42/day, max 4) | 0 | 2 (max 2) |
| wide only, buckets 25 %, rate 20, direction 0.9 | 4 | 3 | 0 | 1 |
| wide only, buckets 40 %, rate 20, direction 0.9 | 3 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| wide only, buckets 50 %, rate 20, direction 0.9 (the default wide kind) | 3 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| wide only, buckets 50 %, rate 10, direction 0.85 | 6 | 2 | 0 | 1 |
| wide only, buckets 60 %, rate 20, direction 0.9 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Every announced episode with the defaults was read: each is a fixed clip (sizes within 0.2 %, 88-100 % one side) or one of the randomised
programs above; none is ordinary flow. Among them: a program near 07:33 UTC most days (4-second gaps for 15-30 minutes: ETH $13.27K buy
Oct 5, $10.80K sell Oct 6, $4.38K buy Oct 8; BTC $27.44K buy Oct 6, $13.24K Oct 7, $3.99K Oct 8, $9.56K Oct 9), an ETH seller of $5.00K
every 3 seconds that came back over two days (Oct 7 12:44 to Oct 9 00:11, with gaps), a BTC $60.00K seller every 2 seconds ($29M in 20
minutes, Oct 5 09:00), the BTC $4.14K spot seller and perpetual buyer of Oct 10 10:08 (one program, a hedge), and a BTC perpetual buyer of
$49.95K every 5 seconds (Oct 10 17:41, $6.3M). Today's ETH buyer: onset 17:12, alert 17:26, still running at 22:00 with $52.5M on its side.

Of the 26 announced, 15 ran at $100K a minute or more and 16 reached $5M: a size floor for sounds (a page setting, scaled with the coin)
would leave about one a day per busy market.

The wide kind's settings: 25 % buckets miss today's buyer's continuity (above) and add nothing found elsewhere; 60 % loses the BTC perp
program; a rate of 10 or a direction of 0.85 adds episodes on ETH spot that are the same sellers' quieter stretches. 50 %, 20 a minute and
0.9 is the default.

Fixes the replay forced (all in the module and its tests): a running episode keeps its side only while at least 75 % of its family's
clips are on it (Argus kept it on any direction, so a busy wide family held one open after its program stopped); a repeat is the same side
and the same clip (it was any side with overlapping sizes, which made the $108M ETH seller of Oct 6 a "repeat" of a $5K buyer); a
fixed-clip episode's numbers come from its exact family only.
